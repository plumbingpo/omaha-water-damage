Flood Damage Preparation Tips in Omaha, NE

Home / Blog / How to Prepare for Flooding in Omaha, NE

Nobody controls the weather, yet Omaha, NE property owners can still get ahead of flood damage with a few proactive steps. Preparation significantly reduces the impact of flooding events and can save thousands in restoration costs. Here Omaha Restoration Co shares the preparation steps that work best for homeowners in Omaha.

Find Out How Flood-Prone You Are

Step one is to learn what flood risk your property actually carries. Check FEMA flood maps to determine whether your property is in a designated flood zone. Being outside a high-risk zone does not make you safe, because most flood claims are filed for properties outside the designated flood zones. Look at how your land slopes, how it drains, and how close it sits to any waterway that might overflow in heavy rain.

Ways to Protect the Property

The main physical steps are to seal cracks in the foundation and apply a waterproof coating, grade the soil so it slopes away from the house, install a battery-backed sump pump and keep it maintained, clear the gutters and downspouts and carry their discharge 6 feet or more away from the foundation, raise HVAC equipment and electrical panels above likely flood levels, and store valuables and important documents above ground level.

Prepare Your Insurance Ahead of Time

A typical homeowner's insurance policy excludes damage from rising floodwater. Consider purchasing flood insurance through the NFIP or a private carrier, and since there is typically a 30-day waiting period before coverage begins, don't wait for a forecast to act. Document your property and belongings with photos and video for insurance purposes. Keep insurance documents accessible and know your coverage limits and deductible.

Your Plan for a Flood Emergency

Make your plan well ahead of any flood threat. Pack a go-bag and learn the routes you would take to evacuate. Keep Omaha Restoration Co's number ((989) 260-9416) in your phone for immediate post-flood professional cleanup. Have your insurance agent's contact information readily available. If flooding is forecast, move valuables to upper levels, turn off utilities if instructed, and document your property's condition before the event.
