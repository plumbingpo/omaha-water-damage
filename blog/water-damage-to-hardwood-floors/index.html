Saving Water-Damaged Hardwood Floors in Omaha, NE

Home / Blog / Saving Water-Damaged Hardwood Floors in Omaha, NE

A hardwood floor adds real value to a home, yet water can damage it more readily than almost any other feature. Cupping, crowning, buckling, staining, and mold in the subfloor beneath are all results of water reaching hardwood. For Omaha, NE homeowners with hardwood floors, it is important to understand how to respond to water exposure and when professional restoration can save your floors versus when replacement is necessary.

What Water Does to Wood Floors

Because wood is hygroscopic, it takes in moisture and gives it back as the conditions around it change. Wood fibers expand as they take on moisture, which is what happens when a floor gets too wet. This causes cupping (edges rising higher than the center of boards), which is the first visible sign of moisture imbalance. Should the wetting go on, crowning may follow (the bottom dries more slowly than the top, so the center rises above the edges), or buckling (boards pull entirely free of the subfloor). When a floor stays wet a long time, mold can also develop under the boards, on the subfloor, unnoticed until they are pulled.

Will Your Wood Floors Survive Water Damage?

Whether water-damaged hardwood can be saved depends on several factors: how quickly professional drying begins, the volume of water involved, how long the wood was wet, the type and finish of the hardwood, and the condition of the subfloor. In many cases, hardwood floors that receive professional drying within 24 to 48 hours of clean water exposure can be saved. Success depends on drying the wood gradually and under control, which lets it give up moisture evenly instead of cracking and splitting.

Professional Hardwood Floor Drying

Wood floors need a specialized approach that differs from the standard methods used on the rest of a structure. Technicians lay panels or drying mats on top of the floor, which sets up controlled conditions and draws moisture out of the boards evenly. Floor drying systems can also be used beneath the subfloor from crawl spaces. The key is gradual, even drying, because drying too rapidly makes the surface dry faster than the core and leads to permanent cupping, cracking, and splitting.

When Replacement Is Necessary

Hardwood floor replacement is typically necessary when floors have been wet for more than 48 to 72 hours, contaminated water (sewage, floodwater) contacted the floors, extensive buckling or delamination has occurred, mold growth is present on the subfloor, or the subfloor itself is compromised.
